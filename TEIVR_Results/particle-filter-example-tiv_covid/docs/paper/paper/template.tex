\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Understanding within-host viral dynamics is fundamental to infectious disease control, enabling prediction of infection outcomes, optimization of treatment strategies, and assessment of transmission risk. Mechanistic compartmental models that track the evolution of target cells, infected cells, and viral particles provide a rigorous framework for quantifying these dynamics. However, translating patient-level viral load observations into parameter estimates for these models presents a formidable computational challenge: traditional Bayesian inference methods such as particle filtering require thousands of forward model evaluations per patient, each potentially lasting minutes to hours when the underlying dynamics are stochastic.

The computational burden of stochastic modeling arises from the need to accurately represent the discrete, probabilistic nature of infection dynamics, particularly during the critical early stages when viral populations are small. While deterministic ordinary differential equation (ODE) models offer computational efficiency, they fail to capture the substantial variability observed in real infections, especially the possibility of early viral clearance or the stochastic timing of immune responses. Hybrid multiscale approaches, such as the Jump-Switch-Flow (JSF) framework, partially address this challenge by adaptively switching between stochastic and deterministic representations based on population sizes. Nevertheless, even with these optimizations, Bayesian parameter inference for a single patient can require hours of computation on modern hardware—an impractical timescale for clinical decision-making or large-scale epidemiological studies.

Neural Posterior Estimation (NPE) offers a transformative solution to this computational bottleneck. Rather than performing Bayesian inference anew for each patient, NPE employs simulation-based inference to train a neural network that directly learns the mapping from observed data to posterior parameter distributions. This amortized inference approach inverts the traditional workflow: a one-time upfront investment in generating training simulations and training the neural network enables near-instantaneous ($\sim$1--5 seconds) posterior inference for any new patient, regardless of the complexity of the underlying stochastic model. Once trained, the same network can be applied to arbitrarily many patients without additional simulation cost, fundamentally changing the feasibility of uncertainty quantification in infectious disease modeling.

Moreover, NPE reveals statistical structures that traditional inference methods often obscure or fail to detect. Particle filters with aggressive resampling can collapse multimodal posteriors into single modes, hiding fundamental parameter identifiability issues. NPE, by contrast, learns to represent the full posterior distribution—including multiple modes, complex correlations, and non-Gaussian structures. This capability is particularly valuable for within-host viral models, where parameters governing viral replication ($\beta$, $\pi$), immune responses ($\phi$), and infected cell clearance ($\delta$) can trade off in complex ways to produce observationally equivalent viral load trajectories. By exposing these identifiability issues, NPE provides critical insight into which parameters can be reliably estimated from available data and where additional measurements are needed.

In this work, we demonstrate the application of Neural Posterior Estimation to within-host SARS-CoV-2 viral dynamics using a multiscale stochastic compartmental model. We employ the Jump-Switch-Flow (JSF) simulation framework to efficiently generate training data that captures stochastic infection dynamics while remaining computationally tractable. We train a neural posterior estimator on synthetic viral load trajectories spanning a wide range of epidemiologically plausible parameters and validate the approach on real patient data from longitudinal COVID-19 studies. Our key contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
	\item We establish an end-to-end NPE workflow for stochastic within-host infection models, reducing per-patient inference time from hours to seconds while maintaining full uncertainty quantification.
	\item We demonstrate that NPE reveals multimodal posterior structures and parameter identifiability issues that are masked by traditional particle filtering approaches, providing deeper insight into model limitations.
	\item We validate the framework on real COVID-19 patient viral load data, showing that NPE-derived parameter estimates are consistent with those from traditional methods while being orders of magnitude faster to compute.
	\item We provide open-source implementations of the JSF multiscale simulator and NPE training pipeline, enabling application to other within-host infection models.
\end{itemize}
